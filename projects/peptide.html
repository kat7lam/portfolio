The research

Peptide amphiphiles combine a hydrophobic tail with a peptide region that interacts with water. In water, they can self-assemble into organized structures. The peptide sequence, charge, and mixture composition offer ways to tune how the assemblies behave.

This project explores VVAA-based peptide amphiphiles bearing lysine (KK) or glutamate (EE) groups. The broader motivation is to develop fluorescence-based sensing systems, including designs intended to bind lead and arsenic. Characterizing the assemblies and their optical behavior is a step toward understanding those potential sensing applications.

My contribution

I characterized the fluorescence of VVAAKK- and VVAAEE-based peptide amphiphiles and mixed assemblies, studying how differences in charge and composition affected their optical behavior.

I first joined Harvey Mudd’s Molecular Engineering Lab during my freshman year, then returned to the project as work on the manuscript progressed. My experimental work focused on fluorescence characterization of the self-assembling systems.

Sharing the research

I presented the research at the 2024 SoCal Soft Matter Symposium at UC Irvine. I am a coauthor on a manuscript that is currently in preparation.
