The B-Flat tortilla machine

E177 Mechanical Design · Harvey Mudd CollegeSpring 2026
Project
Motor-driven tortilla maker
Tools & methods
Mechanical design / Waterjet cutting / 3D printing / Iterative testing
Team
Eduardo Ambriz Lazo, Kate Lam, Andre Mendoza, and Alexa Rosenberg
Read our project site

The idea

Our four-person team built a bench-scale, motor-driven machine to roll masa and cut tortillas. The challenge was to keep the rollers, cutter, and conveyor moving together while producing a consistent shape.

What we built

We combined resin-coated wooden rollers, a 3D-printed cutter, custom waterjet-cut aluminum gears, and a synchronized conveyor. Testing helped us refine the gear train, component fits, and dough handling.

What we learned

We reduced the tortilla diameter from 6 inches to 4 inches to address distorted, oblong output, and used a 1/8-inch roller gap. The project showed how closely the final shape depends on the interaction between the dough and the mechanism.
