The problem

Aquatic photosynthesis depends on environmental conditions that change with depth, including available light and pH. We built an underwater robot that could repeatedly move to commanded depths and collect those measurements in the field.

My contribution

Iterated the mechanical layout and sensor placement through hand sketches, integrated the pressure, light, and pH sensing hardware into the PVC-frame vehicle, and helped waterproof and field-test the system. The robot used closed-loop feedback from the pressure sensor to autonomously navigate to specified depths while continuously collecting data.

Outcome

Deployed the robot at Dana Point from 0.25 m to 1.75 m depth and used the collected light data to calculate an attenuation coefficient of approximately 0.5925 m⁻¹.
