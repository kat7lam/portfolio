Context

Choosing courses across five campuses means balancing requirements, overlapping sections, personal interests, and the time needed to get between buildings. Our four-person team developed optimization models that recommend a semester schedule from a student’s required courses and ranked preferences. We used Spring and Fall 2025 course data and evaluated the models with three simulated student profiles.

My contribution

I gathered data, spoke with people behind an existing scheduling website, and collected stakeholder input on desired features. I also reached out for information about ADA accessibility and the constraints students face when moving between classes, and helped develop the optimization models. As a team, we used a 64-response student survey, input from four professors, and an ADA-coordinator interview to inform the model design.

Model

The AMPL formulation selects courses using binary decision variables. Hard constraints enforce required courses, prevent time conflicts and duplicate sections, and keep the schedule within a specified credit range. Our evaluations used 12–18 credits.

The objective balances ranked humanities interests against major courses. Extensions add penalties for nearby-in-time courses in distant buildings, buildings without accessible entrances, classes outside a preferred time window, and Friday classes. Adjustable weights let the same model represent different priorities.

Tradeoffs

We varied the weights to see when the recommended schedule changed. Stronger time preferences sometimes removed elective courses rather than simply rearranging them. Required courses and the minimum credit load could still prevent a preferred schedule, while the freshman profile had less flexibility because more courses were mandatory.

Accessibility was represented through building information and weighted penalties. The model does not guarantee an accessible route, and a sufficiently valuable course can outweigh an accessibility penalty.

Result

For the math-junior profile, increasing the schedule-shape penalty from 1 to 5 removed two afternoon electives, producing a schedule that finished shortly after noon with no Friday classes. For the freshman profile, the stronger penalty moved a Friday lab to Thursday. The project produced model-generated schedules and sensitivity comparisons that show how preferences interact with academic constraints.
