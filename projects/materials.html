Compositionally-complex lead-free copper-based alloys

The problem

Lead improves machinability in copper alloys through its self-lubricating behavior, but lead-containing wear particles create an environmental problem. Embedding graphite offers a lead-free route to self-lubrication, but carbon is immiscible with copper, making both alloy composition and melting sequence critical to whether graphite can be incorporated at all. The work also explored the cocktail effect: combining multiple alloying elements can produce material behavior that is not predictable from the individual constituents alone.

My contribution

Prepared alloy charges and fabricated samples by hand using an arc melter, iterating both composition and the order in which metals were melted to improve graphite incorporation. Characterized samples using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), and optical microscopy. I then developed a MATLAB image-processing script to quantify carbon content across sample cross-sections, analyzed microstructural trends, and used those results to guide which compositions and processing sequences to test next.

Outcome

Presented the research at the TMS 2026 Conference (The Minerals, Metals & Materials Society). The resulting alloy is being developed for commercial use.

Partnership

Done in partnership with the School of Materials Science and Engineering at the University of New South Wales, Sydney.
